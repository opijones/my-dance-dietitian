Sports Drinks & Creatine: What’s the Deal?

By Sarah Smales · Registered Dietitian · 23 August 2025

Protein shakes, sports drinks and creatine are widely discussed, but dancers need clear context before deciding whether any product is useful.

Start with food

Most dancers can meet their nutrition, strength and performance needs through a varied diet that provides enough overall energy. Supplements cannot compensate for chronic under-fuelling, skipped meals or a limited eating pattern.

Protein shakes

A shake may be convenient when appetite is low, food is unavailable or a quick recovery option is needed. It is not automatically better than food. For recovery, protein is often most useful alongside carbohydrate rather than in isolation.

Sports drinks

Carbohydrate-electrolyte drinks may help during long, intense sessions, hot conditions, heavy sweating or when eating is difficult. For shorter everyday classes, water and regular meals or snacks are usually sufficient.

Creatine

Creatine has evidence for some strength and repeated high-intensity activities, but dance-specific benefits and the right individual context need consideration. It can increase body water and should not be taken simply because it is popular online.

Check before using a product

Supplements can be costly, unnecessary or contaminated. Discuss your circumstances with an appropriately qualified professional, particularly if you are young, have a medical condition, take medication or compete under anti-doping rules.

  • Choose products tested by a recognised programme such as Informed Sport
  • Check whether ordinary food could meet the same need
  • Avoid products that promise rapid or dramatic results
  • Use an individual plan rather than copying another dancer
